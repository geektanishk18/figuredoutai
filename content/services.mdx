Most automation work moves information between tools. Something arrives, something else gets
updated, a notification fires. The information travels faster, but every decision still waits for a
human.

We build the part that decides. These three pieces are how.

## Qualification logic design

**We map how enquiries actually reach you, then encode what makes one worth acting on.**

This is the part almost everyone skips, and the reason most conversational AI projects stall. A
model that has not been told what your business considers a qualified enquiry will produce fluent,
confident, useless output.

We start by mapping the real intake path — every channel, every owner, every point where a
conversation currently stops. Then we work through your criteria with the people who actually apply
them today, usually finding that the criteria exist only as instinct in two or three senior heads
and are applied inconsistently by everyone else.

**What you get:** a documented set of qualification rules — what counts as urgent, what disqualifies,
what escalates, what routes where, and what happens in every ambiguous case. Written in language
your team can read and change.

*This is yours whether or not we build anything further.*

## The conversational decision layer

**A deployed layer that takes first response and acts on those rules.**

It sits in front of your team on the channels your enquiries already arrive through — web, WhatsApp,
phone, forms. It responds immediately, asks the questions your criteria require, and captures
answers as structured data rather than a transcript.

Then it decides. It scores against your rules, prioritises, routes to the person who should own it,
and escalates with context attached. Your team opens an already-qualified conversation.

**What it handles:**

- First response, on every channel an enquiry can arrive through
- The qualifying questions your criteria require
- Structured capture — answers as data, not as a transcript
- Scoring against your rules
- Routing to the person who should own it
- Scheduling, where a slot can be offered
- Escalation, with the context attached

**What it never does:** improvise outside your rules, or make a decision it cannot explain

Every decision is logged with the reasoning behind it. Humans override anything, at any point.

## Operating visibility

**Reporting on what the layer decided, and why.**

A decision system you cannot audit is a liability. This is the layer that makes it inspectable.

You see what arrived, what the system decided, on which rule, what got routed where, what was
escalated, and what humans overrode. Override patterns are the most valuable signal in the whole
system — they show precisely where your written rules and your actual judgement have drifted apart.

**What you get:** a decision log, routing and response-time reporting, override tracking, and a
regular review cycle where we tune rules against what the business has learned.

## When this is not the right fit

We would rather say this before an audit than after a build.

This works when there is **existing enquiry volume** and **qualification criteria that can be
defined**. If enquiries are few enough that your team handles every one properly, a decision layer
solves a problem you do not have.

We are not a lead-generation service. We do not create demand. We decide what happens to the demand
you already have.
