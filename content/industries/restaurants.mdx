A restaurant's enquiry volume is concentrated into exactly the hours when nobody can answer it. The
phone rings hardest at 7:30 on a Friday, which is also when every member of staff is on the floor.

## How intake fails here

- **The line is busy at peak.** Not unanswered — busy. The caller hears a tone and dials the next
  restaurant on the list.
- **Service hours are a blackout.** Messages sent between 7pm and 11pm are read the next morning, by
  which time the evening they were about has passed.
- **Instagram DMs belong to nobody.** They are usually handled by whoever runs the page, who is not
  usually at the restaurant.
- **Large groups need a decision.** A table for fourteen on a Saturday is a yes-or-no that requires a
  manager, and the manager is expediting.
- **Requests get lost between the phone and the floor.** The birthday cake, the high chair, the nut
  allergy — captured on a notepad, transferred imperfectly, occasionally not at all.

Restaurants absorb this because each individual loss is small. The arithmetic is not: a handful of
covers per service, repeated nightly, is a meaningful share of a month.

## What changes

The floor manager arrives at a shift with a table plan rather than a voicemail queue. Bookings that
would previously have gone to whoever picked up first are held while the guest is still in the
conversation.

The layer answers at 7:30 on a Friday because that is a property of the layer, not of how many
people are on shift. And every booking it took, every group it escalated, and every request it
captured is logged against the rule that produced it.
