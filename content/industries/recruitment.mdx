A single field-sales opening in a metro will draw several hundred applications inside a week.

## How intake fails here

- **Volume outruns screening capacity.** Four hundred applications and one recruiter produces
  screening by whatever is at the top of the list, which is chronology, not fit.
- **Good candidates are the fastest to leave.** The people who match your brief are the same people
  holding other offers. A six-day response is a decision made for you.
- **Screening is inconsistent.** Two recruiters applying the same brief reject different people,
  because the brief lives in a job description and the criteria live in their heads.
- **Mandatory credentials are checked last.** A candidate progresses through two rounds before
  someone establishes they do not have the licence, the vehicle, or the right to work the shift.
- **Near-misses are discarded.** Someone wrong for this role but right for one open down the
  corridor is rejected, because nobody is looking across roles.

## What changes

Recruiters stop opening an inbox and start opening a shortlist. The screening that used to happen
inconsistently across four hundred applications happens identically across all of them, in the
minutes after each one arrives.

Candidates hear back the same day, which is the single largest determinant of whether the good ones
are still available when you call. Every screening decision is logged against the rule that produced
it, and when your team overrides one, that disagreement is what tells you the brief needs changing.
