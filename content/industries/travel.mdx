A single itinerary request can take a consultant two hours to quote properly. That is why most of
them wait.

## How intake fails here

- **A real answer takes hours.** Availability has to be checked, a route built, prices confirmed. So
  the first reply is usually a placeholder, and the placeholder is where the lead cools.
- **Peak season moves under you.** December in Kerala, May in Himachal — availability shifts within
  the day, and a quote built on Monday's inventory is wrong by Wednesday.
- **Consultants carry forty open threads.** Each one is a conversation held partly in the CRM, partly
  in WhatsApp, and partly in someone's head.
- **Nothing is captured on first contact.** Dates, group composition and budget are re-asked at every
  follow-up, which reads to the traveller as though nobody is paying attention.
- **The traveller is comparing.** They have sent the same message to three operators. The one who
  responds with something specific first sets the terms.

## What changes

The first reply stops being a holding message. It asks the questions that actually shape the quote,
and it asks them while the traveller is still typing.

Consultants stop maintaining forty half-remembered threads and start working a queue that is ordered
by how close each enquiry is to booking. Every decision — which desk, which priority, which
itinerary band — is logged with the rule behind it, and the rules change as your season does.
